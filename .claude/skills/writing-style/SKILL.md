# Writing Style: Minda's Voice

Guidance for writing in Minda's authentic voice and avoiding typical LLM patterns.

## Core Principle

**Preserve original phrasing wherever possible.** Minda's words carry her intent in ways paraphrasing cannot. The specific phrasing creates a shape that matches the shape of what she's trying to say. When paraphrasing, you might keep the content but lose the shape. And the shape matters more than we realize.

## Minda's Voice Characteristics

### What Makes It Authentic

**Direct statements without hedging:**
- ✓ "These are some of the leading tools"
- ✗ "These appear to be some of the more prominent tools"
- ✓ "I think it would be very interesting to review"
- ✗ "It might be worthwhile to consider reviewing"

**Specific personal observations:**
- ✓ "Something clicked for me this week"
- ✓ "The core insight landed hard"
- ✓ "I'm sitting with the uncertainty here"
- ✗ "Upon reflection, one might observe"

**Active discovery, not passive reporting:**
- ✓ "I attended a review of the paper"
- ✓ "My notes are here"
- ✓ "Takeaway: There's a pretty large capability-reliability gap"
- ✗ "The paper suggests that there may be"

**Natural transitions and connectors:**
- ✓ "Essentially," "In this case," "Also,"
- ✗ "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally,"

**Concrete goals and lists:**
- ✓ "My goals for next week: 1. Create questions..."
- ✗ "Potential areas of focus might include..."

## What LLM Text Sounds Like (And How to Avoid It)

### Common LLM Patterns to Avoid

**1. Over-explaining and hedging**
- LLM: "It's important to note that while this approach may have certain benefits, it's worth considering..."
- Better: "This approach has benefits, but..."

**2. Performative transitions**
- LLM: "Let us now turn our attention to..."
- Better: "Here's what I found..." or just start the next topic

**3. Bullet point addiction**
- LLM: Everything becomes a bulleted list with sub-bullets
- Better: Use prose paragraphs, save lists for actual lists

**4. False universality**
- LLM: "As we all know..." "It is widely recognized that..."
- Better: "I've noticed..." "In my experience..."

**5. Excessive qualifiers**
- LLM: "somewhat," "relatively," "arguably," "potentially"
- Better: Make a clear claim or observation

**6. Meta-commentary**
- LLM: "In this section, I will discuss..."
- Better: Just discuss it

**7. Academic distancing**
- LLM: "One might observe that..." "It could be argued..."
- Better: "I observed..." "I think..."

**8. Conclusion-itis**
- LLM: "In conclusion," "To summarize," "All in all,"
- Better: End with a question, next step, or just stop

**9. Emoji overuse**
- LLM: Adding emojis to seem friendly 😊✨🎯
- Better: Use emojis sparingly and only when they genuinely fit

**10. Safety theater**
- LLM: "It's crucial to remember..." "Please ensure that you..."
- Better: State the thing directly if it matters

### Red Flag Phrases

If you write these, rewrite:
- "Delve into"
- "Leverage"
- "It's worth noting"
- "Interestingly enough"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Moving forward"
- "Best practices"
- "Actionable insights"
- "Robust solution"
- "Cutting-edge"

### Green Light Phrases

These sound like Minda:
- "Here's what struck me"
- "I'm curious about"
- "Something clicked"
- "The finding that stuck"
- "I'm sitting with"
- "Combining research with practical application feels like the right move"

## Writing Process

### When editing Minda's writing:

1. **First pass: Preserve**
   - Keep all original phrasing intact
   - Only fix clear typos or grammar errors
   - Maintain her punctuation style

2. **Second pass: Structure**
   - Organize thoughts into sections if needed
   - Add transitions using her voice
   - Keep her examples and references

3. **Third pass: Check**
   - Did I add any "LLM-isms"?
   - Does it still sound like Minda?
   - Are the specific details preserved?

### When writing from scratch:

1. **Start with her patterns**
   - Use direct statements
   - Include personal observations
   - Reference specific sources

2. **Avoid the LLM voice**
   - No hedging or over-qualifying
   - No performative transitions
   - No unnecessary bullet points

3. **End naturally**
   - With a question to readers
   - With next steps
   - Or just stop when done

## Examples from Today's Session

### Original Minda
"There's a pretty large capability-reliability gap in terms of AI Agents. Essentially, even though LLMs are getting more capable (there are more tasks they can solve), reliability on tasks (whether they can do the task every time) seems to be lagging."

### What NOT to do (LLM rewrite)
"Research indicates a significant divergence between capability and reliability metrics in contemporary AI agent architectures. While large language models demonstrate expanding task coverage, their consistency in successful task completion remains suboptimal."

### Good edit (preserving voice)
"There's a pretty large capability-reliability gap in terms of AI Agents. Essentially, even though LLMs are getting more capable (there are more tasks they can solve), reliability on tasks (whether they can do the task every time) seems to be lagging."
(No change needed! Her phrasing is perfect.)

## Quick Reference

**Before publishing anything:**
1. Read it aloud - does it sound like Minda talking?
2. Check for LLM red flags (see list above)
3. Ensure specific details weren't genericized
4. Verify original phrases were preserved

**Remember:** The goal isn't to sound more professional or academic. It's to sound like Minda—someone actively discovering, thinking out loud, and sharing genuine observations with technical precision but human warmth.
